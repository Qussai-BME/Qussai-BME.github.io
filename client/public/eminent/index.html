Erasmus Mundus Joint Master's

Can a model learn invariance that actually holds on a subject it has never seen?

That's a machine-learning question before it's a biosignal question. My work so far has been testing exactly that, on real cross-subject sEMG data, under a protocol designed to expose failure rather than hide it.

Why this programme

A generalisation question, tested so far, that needs a formal machine-learning setting to go further.

The question

Whether a model has learned something that transfers to a new person, or has simply fit the people it was shown. Everything in this portfolio is built around testing that distinction honestly.

What's been tested so far

  • A metric-integrity benchmark exposing how rest-class prevalence inflates standard accuracy reporting
  • A mechanistic domain-shift analysis explaining why a common adaptation method fails on cross-subject sEMG
  • Open software with a public reproducibility record

Why EMINENT

A joint master's built specifically around advanced machine learning, with the kind of formal training and research environment that would take this from an independent evaluation to a properly developed research direction.

What I'd investigate next

Whether measurement integrity and learned representations can be connected, and whether an adaptive system stays useful once the person using it is new to it.
