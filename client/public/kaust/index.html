Graduate Fellowship — MS / PhD

I want to learn how reliable biosignal models are built when the experiment matters as much as the model.

My current work has taught me to question an attractive accuracy number before trusting it. KAUST is the kind of research-intensive setting where that habit could grow into deeper training in computational methods and neurotechnology.

Why this programme

The habit I want a research-intensive lab to sharpen.

The habit

Question the metric before trusting it, then design the experiment that would break your own result if it's wrong. That discipline runs through every study in this portfolio.

What it has already produced

  • 73-subject, three-database sEMG leave-one-subject-out benchmark
  • An accuracy-versus-macro-F1 divergence reported explicitly for transradial and amputee data, not smoothed over
  • Open repositories and Zenodo archives, so the results are checkable, not just stated

Why KAUST specifically

A research-intensive graduate environment, faculty working on computational neurotechnology and signal processing, and the kind of infrastructure that would let this line of work move from independent evaluation to something with real laboratory depth.

What I want to test next

Whether models that survive strict cross-subject evaluation can be explained, and eventually adapted, for real assistive systems — not just benchmarked.
